SECTION 02

Scaled Dot-Product Attention, Computed Step by Step

At its core, attention is a single formula. Below, a tiny example with 3 words and dk=4 actually computes every intermediate result, step by step (you can check the numbers by hand).

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( QKᵀ / √dk ) V

SECTION 04

Multi-Head Attention: 8 "Viewpoints" in Parallel

The paper uses h=8 attention heads. Each head projects Q/K/V into a different subspace and learns a different kind of dependency. Below are illustrative heatmaps of 4 typical patterns (row = query position, column = key position, brighter = larger weight).

In a real model, each head's pattern emerges from training; research (e.g. Voita et al. 2019) does find interpretable positional heads, syntactic heads and so on. The heads' outputs are concatenated and projected by WO: MultiHead(Q,K,V) = Concat(head₁,…,head₈)WO.

SECTION 05

Positional Encoding: Knowing Order Without Recurrence

Attention by itself is blind to order (permutation-invariant). The paper adds a fixed sine/cosine encoding to each position. Below is the actually computed PE matrix (x-axis = dimension i, y-axis = position pos, dmodel=128, bright = positive, dark = negative).

PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 100002i/d)     PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 100002i/d)

Waveform of a single dimension — dimension i: 20

Low dimensions (left) have short wavelengths and change quickly, encoding local position; high dimensions reach wavelengths of 10000·2π, encoding global position. For any fixed offset k, PE(pos+k) is a linear function of PE(pos), which makes relative positions easy to learn.

SECTION 06

Why Divide by √dₖ?

When dk is large, the dot product q·k has a variance of about dk. The values get large and push softmax into a saturated region where gradients are tiny. Dividing by √dₖ brings the variance back to 1. Drag the slider to feel the difference (left: unscaled; right: scaled. Scores are a fixed random sample scaled to variance dₖ; softmax is computed for real).
